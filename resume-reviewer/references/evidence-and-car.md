# Evidence and CAR Guide

## Evidence Ledger

Record source truth before rewriting:

| Claim | Status | Source | Safe wording | Missing detail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Candidate statement or resume fact | VERIFIED | Resume, note, artifact, or direct answer | Defensible wording | None |
| Plausible but unsupported claim | NEEDS CONFIRMATION | None or incomplete source | Do not use in final | Ask focused question |
| Contradicted, confidential, or invented claim | DO NOT USE | Conflict or privacy restriction | Omit | None |

Do not upgrade `NEEDS CONFIRMATION` through inference. Target job description describes employer need, not candidate history.

## Source Material

Collect useful evidence without judging relevance too early:

- current and prior resumes;
- employer, title, location, month/year dates, and promotion history;
- project briefs, repositories, pull requests, demos, tickets, and deployment links;
- performance reviews, awards, mentor feedback, and recommendations;
- coursework, capstones, research, hackathons, teaching, and volunteer work;
- tests, dashboards, latency, throughput, reliability, adoption, and quality evidence;
- certifications, publications, presentations, and technical communities.

For confidential work, capture approved ranges, percentages, generalized scale, or qualitative scope. Do not copy proprietary code, customer data, secrets, internal URLs, or unreleased product details.

## CAR Extraction

For every role or project, collect:

### Challenge

- What goal, defect, bottleneck, user need, migration, or ambiguity existed?
- What baseline or constraint applied?
- What part belonged personally to candidate?

### Action

- What was built, implemented, tested, integrated, optimized, automated, migrated, analyzed, or deployed?
- Which technologies, methods, or interfaces were actually used?
- Which decisions, tradeoffs, reviews, or collaborations belonged to candidate?

### Result

- What shipped, improved, became faster, passed testing, gained adoption, reduced risk, or enabled future work?
- Which metric, baseline, period, sample size, or comparison supports result?
- Who used or accepted deliverable?

### Scope

- users, requests, records, models, endpoints, components, repositories, regions, or devices;
- latency, throughput, uptime, coverage, error rate, cost, or runtime;
- team size, stakeholders, duration, release stage, or operating environment.

## Evidence Without Metrics

Never invent numbers. Use verified alternatives:

- **Scale:** processed production-like dataset, supported multiple endpoints, covered cross-platform flows.
- **Complexity:** integrated third-party service, handled concurrency, migrated legacy interface, resolved race condition.
- **Quality:** added automated tests, improved observability, passed acceptance criteria, reduced manual steps.
- **Delivery:** deployed working service, shipped feature, completed migration, presented validated prototype.
- **Adoption:** used by project team, accepted by mentor, merged into shared repository, selected for demonstration.
- **Frequency:** automated recurring workflow, supported daily operation, generated repeatable build.

## Core Resume Content

Core Resume is evidence source, not one-job submission. Preserve complete verified material in reverse chronology:

1. Contact information
2. Optional target role family or evidence-backed summary
3. Education
4. Experience
5. Projects
6. Research, publications, awards, or relevant leadership
7. Skills at very end

Retain original facts and fuller bullet inventory. Application Resume selects from this source without overwriting it.
